CLAIM: The 1943 Philadelphia Experiment turned a Navy ship invisible

TL;DR

The Navy's records show no such experiment. The story began with Carl Allen's 1955–56 letters to UFO writer Morris Jessup, was popularized by books in 1965 and 1979, and was formally denied by the Office of Naval Research in 1996. The USS Eldridge was a real ship — decommissioned in 1946, transferred to Greece in 1951, scrapped in 1999.

The legend: in 1943, the U.S. Navy rendered the destroyer escort USS Eldridge invisible — some versions add teleportation and sailors fused into the ship's hull. It is one of the most retold stories in conspiracy lore, with three films (1984, 1993, 2012) and endless forum threads. The Navy's position has never wavered: it never happened.

The paper trail starts with one man. In late 1955, Carl M. Allen mailed an annotated copy of Morris K. Jessup's The Case for the UFO (1955) to the Office of Naval Research, then began writing letters to Jessup himself in early 1956, claiming to have witnessed an invisibility experiment. Jessup's book gave the story its first scaffolding; Vincent Gaddis's Invisible Horizons (1965) and Berlitz & Moore's The Philadelphia Experiment: Project Invisibility (1979) turned it into a bestseller.

The official denials are on the record. In September 1996 the Office of Naval Research stated it 'has never conducted investigations on radar invisibility, either in 1943 or at any other time' — noting dryly that ONR wasn't even established until 1946. No Navy log, photograph, or credible firsthand account has ever corroborated the experiment. In April 1999, veterans who had actually served aboard the Eldridge told a Philadelphia newspaper their ship had never even made port in Philadelphia.

The ship itself had an ordinary, well-documented career: a Cannon-class destroyer escort commissioned August 27, 1943, decommissioned June 17, 1946, transferred to Greece on January 15, 1951 (serving as the Leon until 1992), and sold for scrap in 1999. A real ship, a fabricated experiment.

Evidence status: Disputed — actively contradicted by U.S. Navy records, including the ONR's September 1996 statement; no supporting documentation exists in Navy or NARA files.
